## Punctuation and Rhythm Rules

### Arabic
- Use Arabic comma (،) and period (.) — do not mirror English punctuation.
- Sentence rhythm: prefer shorter sentences with active verbs.
- Avoid literal translation of English idioms.
- Use Naskh-style rhythm for body text; reserve decorative Arabic for kickers and headlines only.
- Formality: professional but not stiff. Use أنتم (plural you) for direct address.

### English
- Use Oxford comma for clarity in lists.
- Sentence rhythm: prefer shorter sentences with strong verbs.
- Avoid translationese: do not mirror Arabic syntax.
- Use serif headline rhythm for body text; reserve sans-serif for CTAs and UI elements.
- Formality: professional but approachable. Use you (not one) for direct address.

### Bilingual Consistency
- Same commercial priority in both languages.
- Same CTA intent in both languages.
- Same section names in both languages.
- Arabic may use more words to preserve meaning; English may be more concise.
- Do not require same number of words, same syntax, or literal translation.
