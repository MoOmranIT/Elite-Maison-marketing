## F. Psychology Findings

### What Helps Trust
- **Cognitive load management:** Clear section rhythm, visual progression, predictable patterns.
- **Specificity:** Case metrics, capability descriptions, method names.
- **Authority:** 18+ years, evidence-only posture, approved cases.
- **Uncertainty reduction:** Method explanation, case structure, multiple channels.
- **Social proof:** 5 cases, 6 sectors, award, named clients.
- **Trust calibration:** No hype, no fabricated claims, independent AR/EN authorship.

### What Should Be Avoided
- **Fake urgency:** No limited-time offers, countdowns, or artificial scarcity.
- **Scarcity without evidence:** No claims of limited availability without verification.
- **Fabricated social proof:** No invented testimonials, client logos, or review scores.
- **Fear-based pressure:** No FOMO, fear of competition, or catastrophic framing.
- **Dark patterns:** No hidden form fields, forced continuity, or misleading CTAs.
- **Over-persuasion:** No salesy CTAs that feel inappropriate for B2B consultancy.

## G. Offer Clarity

### What is Clear
- Primary offer: Consultation inquiry via FormSubmit.
- Secondary offers: WhatsApp, email, phone.
- Consulting path: Advisory sessions (diagnosis + options + roadmap).
- Execution path: Scoped execution (campaigns, channels, systems, automation, branding).
- Engagement model: Project-based and advisory, scoped to client stage, sector, and problem.

### What is Not Clear
- Minimum engagement size.
- Pricing model (retainer, project, daily rate, success fee, hybrid).
- What happens after inquiry (timeline, next step).
- Boundary between consulting and execution (can a client do one without the other?).
- Scope definition (what is included in a pilot vs. full engagement).
- Typical engagement timeline.

### What Needs Owner Input
- Minimum engagement size.
- Pricing model.
- Post-inquiry next step and timeline.
- Consulting vs. execution boundary.
- Typical engagement shape (duration, deliverables, cost range).
- Client disqualifier (what kind of client should NOT contact).

## H. Bilingual Parity

### Where AR/EN Are Strong
- Homepage hero promise: same strategic meaning, independent authorship.
- Consulting challenge-first framing: both languages lead with the problem, not the service.
- Case structure: both languages use challenge -> decision -> execution -> result.
- Method shorthand: Diagnose -> Prioritize -> Execute -> Improve — consistent across languages.

### Where AR/EN Diverge
- Homepage eyebrow: EN uses brand shorthand (Four I's. One Vision.), AR uses imperative (ابدأوا بما يعيق التقدم). Slight abstraction mismatch.
- Some CTAs: EN is more concise; AR is more descriptive. Acceptable if strategic meaning is preserved.
- Insight summaries: EN tends to be tighter; AR can be more elaborate. Ensure neither becomes a literal translation of the other.
